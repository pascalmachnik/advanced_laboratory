A successful lab report should document the experiment in a scientifically correct, transparent, and comprehensible way. 

# Scientific content and theory
Explain the relevant physical theory correctly and use models only within their range of validity. 
State and explain the important equations used in the analysis.
Define the variables, units, assumptions, and, where relevant, the conditions under which an equation is valid. 
Ensure that the theoretical background, analysis, and interpretation are physically consistent.

# Experimental setup and procedure
Describe the experimental setup clearly enough that the measuring principle, relevant components, and their functions can be understood. 
Describe the measurement procedure in a traceable manner. 
State relevant measurement parameters, measurement ranges, instrument settings, and the number and distribution of measurement points. 
Collect sufficient measurement data to support the intended analysis and conclusions. 
Complete and evaluate all tasks required for the experiment.

# Data, units, and uncertainties
Present measured values, calculated quantities, tables, plots, and final results with correct units. 
Identify all relevant input quantities that are subject to uncertainty. 
Justify uncertainty estimates, for example through instrument specifications, reading uncertainty, statistical variation, or systematic effects. 
Propagate relevant uncertainties appropriately to calculated result quantities. 
Use appropriate rounding and significant figures, so that a measured value and its uncertainty are consistent with each other. 
Report results in a form such as 𝑥=(𝑥meas±Δ𝑥)unit, where appropriate.

# Figures, plots, and fits
Give every figure and plot an informative caption that explains its content and, where relevant, the measurement conditions. 
Label each axis clearly with the measured quantity or symbol and its unit. 
Include error bars whenever uncertainties are relevant and can be meaningfully displayed. 
If error bars are smaller than the plotted symbols, state this clearly in the caption or main text. 
Use a fit where it is required by the task or necessary for the scientific analysis. 
State the fit model, the determined parameters, their units, and, where applicable, parameter uncertainties or a goodness-of-fit measure such as 𝜒2. 
# Discussion and interpretation
Discuss the results in relation to expected, literature, or reference values. 
Explain possible deviations between measured and expected results. 
Consider the role of statistical and systematic uncertainties when assessing whether results agree with expectations. 
Draw conclusions only to the extent supported by the data and uncertainty analysis. 

# Structure, writing, and references
Write the report as a coherent and logically structured text. 
Make the reasoning from theory and method to analysis and conclusion easy to follow. 
Use bullet points only where they are appropriate and do not replace necessary explanations and argumentation with lists. 
Cite all sources completely, correctly, and in a way that allows them to be found and checked. 
Verify all cited sources, including references suggested or generated by AI tools.
Do not present text, ideas, structures, data, figures, translations, or references from other sources as your own work. 
Take responsibility for the scientific correctness and verifiability of every statement, calculation, source, and interpretation in the report.
